NotchNook and Boring Notch put music, files and more in the MacBook’s notch. Vortex puts work there: a focus timer, clipboard history, a shelf for files and builds, tasks and text from your screen, and keys Claude Code can use without seeing them. Here’s how the three compare, and when each one is the better pick.

This site is Vortex’s, so read its column with that in mind. Every line about Vortex comes from [its README](https://github.com/OrtexIsArtem/vortex#readme) and [feature docs](https://github.com/OrtexIsArtem/vortex/tree/main/Packages/Features), and every fact about NotchNook and Boring Notch from their own pages, linked under the table.

## Who each app is for

**Vortex** is for people who work on a Mac all day, developers especially. A focus timer, clipboard history and a shelf for files sit one hover away, a shortcut turns anything on screen into a task, Screen Text copies text you can’t select, and Claude Code can use your database URLs and API tokens without seeing them. You add only the features you need. It’s free, open source under MIT, and never goes online by itself.

**NotchNook** is for people who want a notch full of extras and are happy to pay for it. Its site lists music controls, your next meeting with join buttons, a tray for files with AirDrop, clipboard history, notes, timers, a camera mirror and your Shortcuts. Its Pro edition also lists synced lyrics, Pomodoro and to-dos, a file converter, Live Activities and approving Claude Code and Codex from the notch. Pro and Classic each come as a monthly plan or a lifetime license.

**Boring Notch** is for people who want a free, open-source notch built around music: playback controls with a visualizer, the calendar and reminders, a file shelf with AirDrop, and new pop-ups for volume and brightness.

## When NotchNook is the better choice

- **You want music in the notch.** NotchNook has music controls with artwork, and synced lyrics in Pro. Vortex has no media controls.
- **You want your calendar there.** NotchNook shows your next meeting, with join buttons for Meet, Zoom, Teams and Webex. Vortex has no calendar.
- **You want more in the notch:** notes, a camera mirror, your Shortcuts, AirDrop from the Tray and, in Pro, a file converter. Vortex has none of these.
- **You want to approve Claude Code and Codex from the notch.** NotchNook Pro lists it. Vortex asks only about your keys: the first time a Claude Code session uses each one.
- **You have an Intel Mac.** NotchNook Classic runs on Intel too; Vortex needs Apple silicon.

NotchNook is paid: a monthly plan or a lifetime license, each for 2 Macs, after a free trial of 48 hours.

## When Boring Notch is the better choice

- **You want music in the notch, for free.** Boring Notch has playback controls, a visualizer and lyrics. Vortex has no media controls.
- **You want your calendar and reminders there,** and AirDrop from the shelf.
- **You want new pop-ups for volume, brightness and the keyboard backlight.**
- **You want a camera mirror or a battery indicator.**
- **You have an Intel Mac.** Boring Notch runs on Apple silicon and Intel; Vortex needs Apple silicon.
- **You install apps with Homebrew.** Boring Notch has a cask; Vortex comes as a zip from GitHub Releases, or with one command in Terminal.

Like Vortex, it’s free and open source, and macOS asks for one extra step the first time you open it.

## What only Vortex does

Of the three, only Vortex lists these:

- **Keys for Claude Code.** Save a database URL or an API token in Vortex under a name. Claude Code writes `{{name}}` where the value goes, and Vortex runs the command in your shell and takes the value back out of the output. Claude gets the result, never the key, and you allow each key once per session from the notch. [How it works](../claude-code-secrets/).
- **Text from your screen.** Screen Text freezes the screen: frame a screenshot in a chat, a video frame or an error, and its text is on your clipboard, read on your Mac. A QR code gives its link or text.
- **Tasks from your screen.** A shortcut freezes the screen. Frame an area and press Return: the task keeps the picture, with the app, the window title and the page address. Text on it is recognized on your Mac, so search finds the task by any word in the picture.
- **A shelf that catches builds.** Point Vortex at your build folder, and every new .ipa, .apk or .aab lands on the shelf once it’s fully written. Unpinned files leave after a day.
- **Keyboard cleaning.** Its button among Home’s utilities, or a shortcut you set, locks every key, media keys included, while the mouse and trackpad keep working.
- **Offline by design.** Vortex never goes online by itself: no analytics, no update checks, no account. Its one listener is the MCP server for Claude Code, on this Mac only, with a token.
